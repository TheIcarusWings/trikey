How should key 3 work?

Bitkey's third key sits on Block's servers. It signs everyday phone payments under a daily limit and runs the waiting period when you recover a lost device. We want the same experience with no whole key on anyone's server. Below are the options, drawn side by side, then the flows for the leading one.

Already decided

Key 1: any hardware walletLedger, Trezor, Coldcard, BitBox02, Jade, Keystone, Passport, SeedSigner, Krux.
Key 2: phone keyEncrypted backup in any cloud. Unlock with a Recovery Code; a passkey is an optional shortcut.
Script: plain 2-of-3 multisigThe one script every device can sign. Miniscript users can opt into a 1-year timelock backstop.
First app: mobileReact Native, with Ledger Bluetooth and Trezor Connect.

The options

Each diagram shows where key 3's secret lives (red: a whole key someone else holds; green: yours, or only a share) and the path of an everyday phone-only payment.

Reference: Bitkey today

baseline

A · A cosigner on your own device

simple

Good: no third party, mature crypto, works with every hardware wallet.

Bad: not seamless. If the device is off, phone-only payments stop, and a phone thief who also steals the home device gets a quorum.

B · Threshold guardians: key 3 split into shares

leading option

Good: keeps every Bitkey feature and works with every hardware wallet. It needs no setup and no single holder. Two colluding guardians still have only one key of three.

Bad: it uses newer crypto (threshold ECDSA) with few audited libraries, and nobody ships this for bitcoin yet. Replacing a guardian may mean a new key 3 and one on-chain sweep.

C · Several guardians, each with its own key

no new crypto

Good: no new cryptography; any guardian can be replaced by moving coins, and each guardian alone is powerless.

Bad: it breaks our "every hardware wallet" decision, and every payment pays for the bigger script.

D · No key 3, only timelocks in the script

fully keyless

Where users might feel a difference

Connecting the hardware wallet

Bitkey taps over NFC. Ledger Nano X, Stax and Flex and Trezor Safe 7 pair over Bluetooth. Other devices need USB-C (Android) or QR. On iPhone, USB-only devices won't work.

Notifications

Nostr has no push. A small push bridge wakes the app; it sees only an opaque ping. Contacts get alerts as nostr DMs in any nostr app.

Signing speed

Guardians reply over relays in seconds, not Bitkey's sub-second. Pre-computed signatures made when coins arrive close most of the gap.

Who runs the default guardians

"Works out of the box" needs three independent operators ready on day one, for example us plus two partners. That's a business question as much as a technical one.

Decisions for you

  1. Adopt B (threshold guardians) as key 3. Build the guardian protocol, rules and recovery first with a single-key guardian (option A) on testnet only, then switch to shares before mainnet. The wallet script stays the same.
  2. Pick the threshold library. cggmp24 is MIT/Apache and audited (2023), but replacing a guardian means a new key 3 and a sweep. DKLs23 can swap guardians while keeping the key, but it has a non-commercial licence and parts of it are unaudited. Either way, plan an independent audit.
  3. Default guardian operators: who they are, and whether users pay them (Lightning, per wallet).
  4. Keep option A as an advanced mode ("be your own guardian") alongside B?
