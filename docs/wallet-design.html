The three keys

Which two keys, when

The daily limit, without a server

Bitkey's server checks every phone-only payment against a limit. Here, the phone spends from its own small balance, and the vault feeds that balance through refills you sign in advance. Each refill is locked to the one before it.

What you trade for having no third party

One more thing to keep

The Recovery Kit has to live somewhere other than the seed words. Bitkey avoids this only because its server holds key 3, which is the trust we're removing.

No undo window

If someone gets two of your keys, nothing makes them wait. In return, losing one device never needs anyone's approval.

Refill upkeep

A few on-device confirmations every couple of months, plus a fresh batch after big payments.

On-chain footprint

Each refill is a real transaction with a fee. Weekly refills keep that small; daily ones would mean more fees and more confirmations.

Decisions for you

  1. Default Recovery Key: printed Recovery Kit, an NFC card you tap (closest to Bitkey's feel), or a second hardware wallet.
  2. Default refill: amount and rhythm, for example 0.002 BTC weekly with 8 refills per batch. Users could change these later on the hardware wallet.
  3. Phone key cloud backup: on by default (no sweep needed after losing the phone) or off (one less copy of a key anywhere).
  4. Where the app watches the chain: a public server (it sees your addresses, but can't touch funds) or your own node.

To check before building: that every target hardware wallet signs refill transactions with a custom relative timelock (Trezor in particular), and which NFC cards work as a Recovery Key.
