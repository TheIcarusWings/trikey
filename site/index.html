The three keys

Each key has one job.

  1. Key 2

    Your phone

    Everyday payments with Face ID. The key sits behind your phone's biometrics, with an encrypted backup in your own cloud that a passkey unlocks.

  2. Key 1

    Any hardware wallet

    Ledger, Trezor, Coldcard, Jade, Keystone and other multisig devices. For big payments, changing your rules, and recovery.

  3. Key 3

    Trikey Guardian

    An app on your own Umbrel or Start9. It co-signs phone payments under a daily limit and runs a 7, 14 or 30-day Delay & Notify recovery.

Under the hood it's a plain wsh(sortedmulti(2,…)) descriptor, so any multisig wallet can recover your funds without Trikey.

Your home server

Your phone and hardware wallet have you.

It dies or goes offline?

Your phone and hardware wallet still move any amount. Phone-only payments wait until the guardian is back, or you set up a new guardian and move to a fresh wallet.

Stolen?

It locks itself after any restart: key 3 stays encrypted until you unlock it. Unplugged, it's useless to them. Think it's been tampered with? Lock it from your phone. And it never held your money. Just one key of three.

Under the hood

Nobody in the middle. Not even us.

Your phone and your guardian never talk directly, and never through a server of ours. They pass encrypted notes through nostr relays, and both only ever dial out.

01 Your own node
The guardian reads the chain from the Bitcoin node and Electrs on your own box. No public servers.
02 Outbound only
Nothing opens a port on your home network. Relays see only ciphertext: they can drop a message, but not read or forge one.
03 Recover anywhere
A plain wsh(sortedmulti(2,…)) descriptor on standard paths. Any multisig wallet can move your coins with two of your keys.
04 Open source
MIT licensed. The wallet, the guardian, the design and the threat model are all public.

Questions

Straight answers.

More in the design and threat model.

Is Trikey ready to use?

No. It's pre-alpha and hasn't been audited, so don't use it with real funds. It runs on testnet, signet and regtest while it's built, and there's no release to download yet. An independent security review is planned before mainnet.

Does anyone at Trikey hold one of my keys?

No. All three keys are yours. Nobody runs a server, a guardian or a cosigner for you: the guardian is an app on your own home server.

What if Trikey goes away?

Your wallet is a plain wsh(sortedmulti(2,…)) descriptor on standard BIP 48 paths, and your phone's backup holds it. Any multisig-capable wallet can recover your coins with two of your keys, no Trikey needed.

Do I need a home server?

No. Recovery Key mode keeps key 3 offline, on a printed Recovery Kit or a second hardware wallet, and gives the phone a small spending balance refilled by timelocked, pre-signed refills. Recovery then takes any two keys, with no waiting period.

Which hardware wallets work?

Ledger and Trezor over Bluetooth, and QR devices such as Coldcard, Keystone, Passport, Jade and SeedSigner.

Can the nostr relays see my wallet?

No. Every message is end-to-end encrypted to the other side. A relay can drop messages, but it can't read or forge them. You can use several relays, or run your own.

What if someone hacks my home server?

They get one key of three: the same exposure as a company's cosigning server being breached. They still need a second key to move anything, and the guardian only co-signs phone payments under your limit.
