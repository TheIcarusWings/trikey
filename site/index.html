Your keys. Your server. Your bitcoin.

Trikey is a 2-of-3 bitcoin wallet that feels like Bitkey, with no company holding a key. Your phone, your hardware wallet and your own home server: any two move your money.

For years, holding bitcoin meant a choice.

One key. One mistake. Gone.

A third way

What if you held three keys?

  1. One in your pocket.

    Key 2 Your phone

    Everyday payments with Face ID. The key sits behind your phone's biometrics, with an encrypted backup in your own cloud that a passkey unlocks.

  2. One in a drawer.

    Key 1 Any hardware wallet

    Ledger, Trezor, Coldcard, Jade, Keystone and other multisig devices. For big payments, changing your rules, and recovery.

  3. One on a shelf at home.

    Key 3 Trikey Guardian

    An app on your own Umbrel or Start9. It co-signs phone payments under a daily limit and runs a 7, 14 or 30-day Delay & Notify recovery.

Any two move your money.

Under the hood it's a plain wsh(sortedmulti(2,…)) descriptor, so any multisig wallet can recover your funds without Trikey.

How it feels

Quick when it's small. Careful when it's big.

Lose one? The other two have you.

Anything suspicious, you and the people you trust hear about it. Every day.

A short series

If a key goes missing

Part 1 · Key 2

Your phone

Lost it?

Got your backup? Restore it on a new phone with your passkey. No waiting.

No backup? Your hardware wallet and guardian start a recovery. It waits 7, 14 or 30 days and alerts you and your contacts every day. Any of you can cancel a fake one.

Stolen?

They can spend at most your daily limit. Raising it needs your hardware wallet. Start a recovery and phone-only payments pause.

Part 2 · Key 1

Your hardware wallet

Lost it?

Restore your seed words on a new device. Done.

Stolen?

It's PIN-locked. And even unlocked, it's one key of three. If they try to start a recovery with it, you and your contacts are alerted every day, and you cancel it.

Then restore your seed words and move everything to a fresh wallet with your phone. Keep the seed words somewhere other than the device, and outside your home.

Part 3 · Key 3

Your home server

It dies or goes offline?

Your phone and hardware wallet still move any amount. Phone-only payments wait until the guardian is back, or you set up a new guardian and move to a fresh wallet.

Stolen?

It locks itself after any restart: key 3 stays encrypted until you unlock it. Unplugged, it's useless to them. And it never held your money. Just one key of three.

One key gone. Your bitcoin isn't.

What it stands for

  • No company holds a key.
  • No server but yours.
  • No one to ask permission.
Your own home server
The guardian runs on your Umbrel or Start9 and reads the chain from your own node. No public servers.
Outbound only
Phone and guardian talk over end-to-end encrypted nostr relays. Nothing opens a port on your home network, and relays see only ciphertext.
No home server?
Recovery Key mode keeps key 3 offline (a printed kit, an NFC card or a second hardware wallet) and gives the phone a small spending balance with timelocked refills.
Open source
MIT licensed. The wallet, the guardian, the design and the threat model are all public.

Coming soon. Built in public.

Pre-alpha and not audited. Don't use it with real funds. There's no release yet, so there's nothing to download. It runs on testnet, signet and regtest while it's built. An independent security review is planned before mainnet. Know the trade-offs too: a remote compromise of your home server gives up one key of three, and phone-only payments wait while the guardian is offline.
